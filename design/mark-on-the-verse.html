Where a mark belongs

Psalm 23, with verse 4 marked and verse 1 carrying a written note, so the two can be told apart at a glance.

You are right, and it is a model mistake rather than a styling one. A note is appended to a verse: you read the words and they tell you what they belong to. A mark has no words, so putting it in the same place borrows the note's structure and delivers none of its meaning — a row that takes up the space of a thought without containing one. A mark is not a thing below the verse. It is a property of the verse. That is what highlighting means on paper, and it answers "what does it belong to" by construction, because the verse is the mark. It also makes the progression clean: mark a verse now, write something later, and it moves from the text into the note rail on its own.
AColoured verse number

The lightest possible touch. Nothing happens to the scripture itself; only the number beside it changes.

1 The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.

A shepherd metaphor from a man who had actually kept sheep.

2 He makes me lie down in green pastures; He leads me beside quiet waters.
3 He restores my soul; He guides me in the paths of righteousness for the sake of His name.
4 Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for You are with me.

For: completely leaves scripture alone; unmistakably a property of the verse. Against: very easy to miss, and on mobile the number is tiny.

BSoft tint behind the verse

The closest thing to a highlighter pen, and the one most people will already understand without being told.

1 The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.

A shepherd metaphor from a man who had actually kept sheep.

2 He makes me lie down in green pastures; He leads me beside quiet waters.
3 He restores my soul; He guides me in the paths of righteousness for the sake of His name.
4 Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for You are with me.

For: instantly legible, needs no explanation, scales to a whole page of marks. Against: it collides with the existing selection and search-jump washes, which already tint a verse. Three different tints on one screen would be one too many.

CMargin rule beside the verse

A coloured line in the margin, which is what people actually do in a paper Bible. Shown here with verse 1 also marked, in a different category, to show two side by side.

1 The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.

For: reads as a margin annotation, which is exactly what it is; survives a multi-verse span cleanly; does not touch the scripture or fight the selection wash. Against: the selected state already uses an inset left rule in the accent colour, so the two would need to be told apart.

DUnderline the words

A tinted band under the text, like a soft highlighter stroke.

1 The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want.

A shepherd metaphor from a man who had actually kept sheep.

4 Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for You are with me.

For: the most "I marked this" of the four. Against: it is the only one that touches the scripture itself, and it fights the reading rhythm on wrapped lines.

And the Journal needs changing too

Today a mark shows as a row with the word "Marked" in it, which has the same problem you described: it occupies a note's space and says nothing. Better is to show it as what it is, with no word at all.

23:1 A shepherd metaphor from a man who had actually kept sheep.
23:4 Marked
23:4

Second row is today. Third is the proposal: the reference, and a short rule in the category colour. Nothing pretending to be prose.

What I would do

  • C, the margin rule. It is the only one that says "this is my mark on the page" without touching the scripture or competing with the two tints the reader already meets (selection, and the search-jump highlight). It is also what the rail bracket already does for multi-verse notes, so marks and notes would read as the same family — which they are.
  • B is the popular answer and I would still not pick it, purely because of the tint collision. If you would rather have the familiarity, the honest cost is re-designing the selected and jumped-to states so three washes stay distinguishable.
  • Either way, nothing goes in the note rail for a mark. That is the part that matters, and it is your point.
