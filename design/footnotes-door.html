The footnotes door

Rung one of the deep dive: the translators’ own “Or…” and “Literally…” notes. Every verse and every note on this page is real BSB data, fetched from the live API, not written for the mockup. docs/proposals/footnotes-door.md decided the data and the rules; this decides what it looks like.

1. The thing the brief could not settle from the data

The API marks a note’s position, not its span. A marker says “a note ends here” and never says where the phrase it refers to began. So the brief’s underline has no start, and one has to be inferred. Real John 1:

v5  “…the darkness has not overcome● it.”  →  Or comprehended
v14 “…made His dwelling among us.●”  →  Or and tabernacled among us
v18 “…is at the Father’s side,●”  →  Greek in the Father’s bosom

Three ways to turn a point into a span. Same verses, same notes, all three rendered:

A · last wordalways one word
…the darkness has not overcome it.  ·  …made His dwelling among us.  ·  …is at the Father’s side,
B · match the notespan = the alternative’s length
…the darkness has not overcome it.  ·  …made His dwelling among us.  ·  …is at the Father’s side,
C · to the clauseback to the last comma or stop
…the darkness has not overcome it.  ·  …made His dwelling among us.  ·  …is at the Father’s side,

My recommendation is B, with A as the fallback. A is honest and never looks broken, but “us” is a meaningless door for a note about tabernacled, and roughly a third of notes land on a function word. C is the prettiest and the most confidently wrong — it underlines words the note has no opinion about. B underlines about as much text as the note offers to replace, which is the honest claim: this much could have been said differently. When B cannot align (the note is a gloss, not a substitution) it degrades to A. B is also the only one that needs a real accuracy pass, so if that worries you, A ships and is never embarrassing.

2. How heavy the underline is

The brief settled the kind of mark: a dotted hairline, no superscript numeral, no dot, no colour, no icon. Colour is spoken for — it means “I said something”, and the reader’s own marks must win that. What is left is weight. Toggle the theme; this is the decision that most often survives light and dies in dark.

4. What the popover says, and in whose voice

Left: the note bare. Right: the note with a quiet attribution line. The brief is silent on this and it is the one place the feature could start sounding like it has an opinion.

Or and tabernacled among us

Nothing but the data. Shortest, and it never editorialises. But an unlabelled “Or…” floating over scripture does not say who is offering the alternative — and a reader could reasonably think it is Lantern.

Translators’ note · BSB
Or and tabernacled among us

Six words of chrome that make the source explicit and put the sentence in someone else’s mouth. This is on-philosophy: mediated by method, and visibly not Lantern’s voice. My recommendation.

5. Mobile, where the tap is already spoken for

On mobile a tap on a verse starts a selection. The brief’s precedence: a live selection wins and markers go inert; with no selection, a tap on the phrase opens the note and a tap anywhere else selects; a drag never opens a note. Both states, side by side.

No selection → the tap opens the note. Bottom sheet, not a floating popover: 390px has no room beside anything. No keyboard, no scroll jump.

Selection active → the underline is gone entirely, not merely inert. Capture owns the screen. Showing a door you have disabled is worse than showing no door.

This is the one thing a mockup cannot settle. Whether the phrase is reliably tappable without stealing taps from verse selection needs a thumb on a real device. The brief says so too. Worth doing before this ships, not after.

6. Three questions the mockup cannot answer

Should textual variants ever ship? The brief holds them back entirely — no marker, no count, genuinely silent — and that is more conservative than the earlier deep-dive plan. On this page, John 1:18 is the live example: it carries “BYZ and TR but the only begotten Son” and shows nothing at all. I would hold. A reader with no way to weigh a variant is not informed by it, only unsettled. Relaxing this later is easy; un-ringing it is not.

Does the reader need an off switch? The measured density says no: median two per chapter, 274 chapters get none. I would ship without one and add it if somebody asks, rather than pre-emptively adding a setting that mostly advertises that the feature might annoy you.

A way to report a note that reads wrong? The ship set’s variant rate is under 2.5% but not zero, so some will slip through. I would skip it in rung one. You are the only reader who will notice for now, and a report button on scripture is a heavier thing to put on a page than it looks.
