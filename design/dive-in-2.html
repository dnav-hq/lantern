2 · The view

Verse held, the passages that pick it up, where it happens. The map is the parchment relief you chose (treatment a: the shipped hillshade under a hypsometric ramp, clipped to the coast polygons), framed tight on the chapter, with labels sized to the frame. A row is the reference, the BSB heading it sits under in grey, and two lines of the passage with the shared words marked. No author, no relation words: the marks say "quotes" and "names Damascus too" without saying it, and they make no claim about direction. The map is the chapter's own places on parchment, with a bearing or two kept faint so the reader is never lost.

2b · Follow, and come back

Two depths, one tap each. Tap a row and it opens in place: the whole passage, marks kept, nothing else moves. That answers most curiosity without leaving the verse. The open row offers one more step, "Read it in James 2", which puts the chapter itself in the sheet with the passage lit and a Back that returns to exactly this view. Both are live in these phones.

3 · The journey

One leg at a time, with its verse. The whole route stays faint; one leg is lit, with the only arrowhead on the map; the caption is that leg's verse with the two places marked. Two controls to judge: A, tap the map to step; B, a strip of the stops under the map, where tapping a stop lights the leg that reaches it and the strip itself shows the shape of the journey, silent stretch included. No numbers, no gradient, no list: a route that doubles back is no harder than one that does not, because only one leg is ever lit. The silent fourteen years are the dotted leg, with the note the data carries for it.

What this drops from the first design.

  • The context-line classifier and the 66-book "who" table. The marks carry the relation; the reference carries the who.
  • The glimpse strip. Replaced by one line of real content with a count.
  • The verse-opens transition. The chapter never leaves; the shipped sheet motion stays.
  • Numbers, visit pairs, gradient, draw-in and the step list. Replaced by one lit leg and its verse.
  • The sea band. Coastlines are clipped as polygons, so the fill closes along the frame edge instead of a chord across the land.
  • Region stops that sit on a city. OpenBible places "Syria" on Damascus and "Judea" on Jerusalem; a region stop at a city's exact point is drawn as a label on the next leg, never as a return to the city.
