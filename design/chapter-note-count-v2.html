Design ideation · round two

Permutations of the corner

Three variants of the option you liked, plus the one that might replace it entirely. All on Romans 8, twelve notes.

Where I was wrong. I ranked prominence by usefulness. The correct inverse is that because nobody really cares about the number, it should be peripheral, not gently inline — and putting a line under the book title, in the reading column, greets you before the scripture does. The corner keeps the reading column clean, which is the stronger position. What I would still hold onto is much narrower: not the corner, just the bare number leading. "12" reads as a tally. Dots first, with the count second or absent, reads as "something of yours is here".
2aDots only — no number at all

The dots say what kinds of notes are in the chapter. Nothing is counted. This is the version that cannot become a scoreboard, because there is no score.

Romans
16 chapters
6 7 8 9 10

28 And we know that God works all things together for the good of those who love Him, who are called according to His purpose.

29 For those God foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son.

For: the quietest thing that still answers the question, and it carries real information (what kind, not how much). Against: three small dots may be too subtle to notice at all.

2bDots, count second and faint

Your original pick, with the reading order reversed so the qualitative signal leads and the number is a detail rather than a headline.

Romans
16 chapters
6 7 8 9 10

28 And we know that God works all things together for the good of those who love Him, who are called according to His purpose.

29 For those God foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son.

For: unmistakable, still out of the reading column, and "12 notes" reads as a fact where "12" reads as a score. Against: the most visually present of these three.

2cThe chapter strip answers it instead

This is the one I would actually argue for now, and it came out of your push. A pip under the chapter numbers you have written in. It answers the question at the moment you are choosing where to go, which is when it is genuinely useful — rather than telling you about the chapter you are already in, which the notes beside the verses already show you. Note chapters 8 and 10 are marked here.

Romans
16 chapters

28 And we know that God works all things together for the good of those who love Him, who are called according to His purpose.

29 For those God foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son.

For: nothing new is added to the page at all; an existing control gets slightly more honest. No number anywhere. Answers "where have I been working" across the whole book at a glance. Against: it does not give you a way to jump to the Journal, so it may need 2a alongside it.

Where I land now

2c, with 2a beside it if a jump-off is wanted. Your instinct that this may not need to exist at all was the useful part: the reading page already shows notes beside the verses, so a header count mostly restates what scrolling reveals. The genuinely unanswered question is which chapters have I written in, and that belongs on the chapter strip, where you ask it.

  • No number anywhere in 2c. Presence, not quantity — which sidesteps the scoreboard problem rather than styling around it.
  • It reuses a control that already exists, so the page gains no new element. That is the cheapest possible answer to "does this break the calm".
  • Still one link, no new surface. Anything clickable here goes to the Journal filtered to the book, which now exists.
  • Open question for you: should the pip be category-coloured, or always neutral? Coloured is more informative and slightly busier on a long book like Psalms.
