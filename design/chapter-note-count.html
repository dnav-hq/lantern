Design ideation · needs your call

How does a chapter say it already has notes?

"Everything I have ever written about this chapter" already works — every note anchored in the chapter renders in the reading view, whichever passage row stores it. The only thing missing is a way to know that before you scroll. Three ways to say it, all on Romans 8 with twelve notes.

The tension. A count is genuinely useful: it is the difference between "somewhere in here I wrote something" and knowing. But a number on a page of scripture is also the first step toward a page that keeps score, and the whole project is committed to no streaks, no metrics, no sense of a tally. So the question is not which looks nicest. It is how much presence a number earns on a page whose job is to be quiet.
1 Under the chapter title

Sits where the chapter count already sits, in the same register. Reads as part of the heading rather than as an annotation on the text.

Romans
16 chapters
67891011

28 And we know that God works all things together for the good of those who love Him, who are called according to His purpose.

29 For those God foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son.

For: quiet, expected, easy to ignore. Against: it is the first thing under the book name, so it greets you before the text does.

2 A corner chip, with categories

Off to the side rather than in the reading column, and it carries a little more: the colours say what kind of notes are in there, not just how many.

Romans
16 chapters
67891011

28 And we know that God works all things together for the good of those who love Him, who are called according to His purpose.

29 For those God foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son.

For: out of the reading column entirely; the category dots are real information. Against: a bare number in a chip is the most scoreboard-like of the three, and it competes with the chapter strip for the same corner.

3 At the end, after the reading

Nothing at the top at all. The chapter ends, and then it tells you. This is the only option that respects observe-before-consult, which every study method teaches and which the deep dive is already built around.

Romans
16 chapters
67891011

28 And we know that God works all things together for the good of those who love Him, who are called according to His purpose.

29 For those God foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son.

You have written twelve notes on this chapter.

For: the reading page stays completely clean; the sentence is warmer than a number. Against: you only learn it after scrolling, which is exactly the thing this was meant to fix.

What I would pick, and why it is still your call

  • Option 1, with one change: no number until it matters. Show a plain "you have notes here" for small counts and only quantify past, say, five. A count of 3 is noise; a count of 30 is genuinely useful information about a chapter you have lived in.
  • Option 3 is the most on-philosophy and I still would not choose it alone. Observe-before-consult argues for it, but the point of this affordance is knowing before you scroll. It could be added as well, cheaply.
  • Option 2 is the one I would avoid. A bare number in a pill is the closest any of these comes to a scoreboard, and that is the line the project has drawn.
  • All three link to the same place: the Journal, filtered to that book — which now exists, so this is a link, not a new screen. No third surface rendering the same notes.
