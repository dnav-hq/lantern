The map, as a doorway

One screen of Genesis 12, to argue three things: that the map is a door you open rather than a panel that is always there, that geography is a lens on the text rather than a picture beside it, and that admitting what scholars do not know is a feature.

Honesty note. Place coordinates below are real, to roughly the precision OpenBible publishes. The coastline and contours are illustrative placeholder geometry drawn for this mockup and are not accurate. In the real build they come from Natural Earth via the build-time ETL in docs/proposals/bible-map-v1.md. Nothing here should be treated as authentic cartography yet.

What this mockup is arguing

  • The map is behind a door, not always on. It opens from one line in the reader's own words, and only because Genesis 12 actually has geography worth opening. A chapter with no places shows no door.
  • The door names the payoff, not the feature. "Follow Abram's journey · about 1,000 km on foot" primes a question. "Map" would not. The distance is the hook, because the thing most readers have never felt is how far this was.
  • Disputed locations look disputed. A dashed open ring and an italic label, not a confident pin. 58% of biblical places have more than one proposed location, so this is the normal case and the design has to carry it without apology.
  • Provenance is visible. Every place links back to its OpenBible entry. No competitor bothers, and it is the cheapest possible proof that we did not invent the geography.
  • Geography points back at the text. Tapping a place gives you the verses set there, not an encyclopedia entry. The map is a way into scripture, not a destination.

Open questions for you

1. How hand-made should it look? This is deliberately restrained: paper grain, two tones of land, a few contour strokes. The alternative is a much more drawn, illustrative style with hachured hills and lettered regions, closer to a printed study Bible plate. More beautiful, considerably more work, and harder to keep legible on a phone.

2. Does the journey line belong here at all? It is the most striking thing on the screen, and it is also the most interpretive: sequencing places into a route is a reading of the chapter. Genesis 12 is explicit about the order, so it is safe here. Many chapters are not.

3. Labels on the map, or only in the panel? Labels make it feel like a real map and are the first thing to break on a small screen.

4. Is the distance figure earned? "About 1,000 km" is computed from the coordinates, so it is honest as straight-line distance between points, but nobody walked in straight lines. Say "as the crow flies", or drop the number?
