The category palette

Ten slots with a tuned light and dark value each, so a colour cannot read in one theme and fail in the other. Every value below is solved, not chosen — the numbers on each swatch are measured against all twenty theme canvases Lantern ships.

The ten slots

Click one to try it. The four naming a category underneath are today’s built-ins, at exactly their current hexes — nothing about the existing look changes. The other six are new.

How it looks where it actually appears

The same slot on a highlighted verse, on a note card, and in the picker. This is the whole reason for a curated set: the tint, the rail and the small uppercase label all have to hold at once, in both themes.

The rule each pair has to satisfy

Stated so this is repeatable rather than a matter of taste, and so a future slot can be added without re-litigating it. Measured against every --bg and --surface in tokens.css, which is 7 light canvases and 13 dark ones including the pure-black variants.

Field (fills, dots, rail, left-border) ≥ 3:1 on every canvas — WCAG 1.4.11 non-text contrast. Ink (the 12px/500 label) ≥ 4.5:1 on the canvas and on the category’s own 12% tint, since the tint is what it actually sits on. Dark doubles as both, because ink aliases to the field in dark mode, so it carries the stricter 4.5:1 bar. No two slots within 25° of hue.

One deliberate exception. Slate sits 12° from blue, well inside the hue rule. It is kept because it is separated by saturation instead (18% against 55%), and a quiet grey-blue is the one thing ten saturated hues cannot offer. It is the only slot that breaks the rule and it should stay the only one.

Why there is no hex field

A colour picker cannot express the constraint. Every value here needed three solved numbers and twenty measurements; a person with a colour wheel picks one. The failure is silent and one-sided — you choose a colour in light mode, it looks right, and your labels quietly become unreadable for everyone reading in dark. Storing a slot id ("teal") rather than a hex is also what lets a pair be retuned later if a theme changes.
