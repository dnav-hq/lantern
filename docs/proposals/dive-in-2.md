# Dive in, second design: one sheet, three things, nothing new to read

*2026-09-14. A cold critique of `design/dive-in.html` and the live app, then a
counter-design. Mockup at `design/dive-in-2.html`, generated by
`scripts/export-dive-in.mjs` from the shipped bundles and helloao, so every
row, heading, marked word, place, coastline and leg is what the data draws.
The page is interactive: tap the line under Genesis 15:6; tap the Galatians
map to step the route.*

## The critique, most damaging first

1. **The first mockup promises data the app does not have.** Galatians 1:17's
   top cross-reference score is 4 and the live gate is 30, so the app shows no
   connections there. The mockup showed two.
2. **The rows repeat themselves.** Five of eight context lines on Genesis 15:6
   say "quoting this word for word"; three begin "Paul, writing to the". Three
   to four lines per row to say one thing eight times.
3. **"Quoting" is a claim.** It asserts direction and dependency, and it is
   false for Galatians 1:17 to 1:18 (one sentence split across two verses).
4. **The sea band, real cause.** The thumbnail fills each *clipped, open*
   coastline run as land; a filled open path closes with a straight chord
   between its ends. Construction, not data.
5. **The route uses five devices for one problem** (numbers, visit pairs,
   gradient, arrowheads, dotted legs, draw-in, a step list). The live map is
   worse: seven stops, a stray "5" beside Damascus, two overlapping legs.
6. **The glimpse does not pull.** Clipped grey italics read as a caption cut
   off by a layout bug, and it has no verb.
7. **The verse-opens transition costs the page.** Collapsing every sibling
   verse turns "come back" into navigation.
8. Small: the full map's confidence legend shows dataset-wide counts; the
   provenance paragraph is the longest block in the view.

One correction to the critique as first delivered: the Genesis 15 thumbnail's
"Ur of the Chaldeans" and "the Euphrates" are real shipped places for that
chapter. The live map hides Ur because it shares Chaldea's exact point.

## The design

**Entrance.** One line under the chosen verse, and it is real content: the
strongest passage with the words the two verses share already marked, and a
count. A journey chapter leads with the route instead. A verse with nothing
beneath shows nothing. Tap: the shipped sheet rises; the chapter stays.

**The view.** Verse held with its marks. Rows: reference, the BSB heading in
grey italic, two lines of the passage with the shared words marked. No author,
no relation words. The marks say "quotes" and "names Damascus too" without
saying it, and make no claim about direction. Then the map card: the chapter's
places on parchment, a bearing or two kept faint, one caption. Then the fold
and one provenance line. Journey chapters put the map first.

**The journey.** The whole route stays faint. One leg is lit, with the only
arrowhead on the map. The caption is that leg's verse with the two places
marked. Tap to step. A route that doubles back is no harder than one that
does not, because only one leg is ever lit. The silent stretch is dotted and
carries the note the data holds for it.

**Desktop.** The same sheet as a panel beside the text.

## Bounded by the data

- **Gate.** Open the door when the top score clears 30 *or* the top passage
  is a parallel account: it names a place this chapter names, measured on the
  shipped place list. Galatians 1:17 opens on Acts 9:20-25 this way.
- **Shared places** are the chapter's own OpenBible places that appear in
  both texts. Nothing is inferred; a name not in the place bundle is never
  marked.
- **Row window.** When the shared run sits deep in a passage, the row starts
  at the nearest opening quote, sentence or clause before it, prefixed with an
  ellipsis, so two clipped lines still show the connection.
- **Region stops on a city's point.** OpenBible places "Syria" on Damascus and
  "Judea" on Jerusalem. A region stop at a city's exact point is folded into
  the next leg ("Jerusalem to Cilicia, through Syria"), never drawn as a
  return to the city. The folded leg keeps the later citation, which names the
  destination.
- **Ranged citations** show the arrival verse (Galatians 1:17-18 shows 1:18).
- **Same-point places** collapse to one label; two regions join ("Chaldea,
  Ur"), a settlement wins over a region.
- **Coastlines are clipped as polygons** (Sutherland-Hodgman against the
  frame), so the fill closes along the frame edge.

## What this drops from the first design

The context-line classifier and the 66-book "who" table; the glimpse strip;
the verse-opens transition; numbers, visit pairs, gradient, draw-in and the
step list; the hand-authored thumbnail frames.

## What changes in code, if approved

- `VerseDoorways` becomes the one entrance line (journey, else strongest row,
  else places). Presence and content come from the loaders already shipped.
- `DeepDiveSheet`/`ConnectionsDoor`: rows drop the quotes/echoes label for the
  marks (already computed by `sharedRun`) plus shared-place marks; the row
  window; the heading moves onto the reference line.
- `connections.ts`: `doorOpens` gains the parallel-account clause; a
  `sharedPlaces(chapterPlaces, source, target)` helper, unit-tested.
- A pure-SVG `MapThumb` from the shipped bundles with polygon clipping done at
  build time into the base bundle (or at render time from the same paths).
- `buildJourneyRoute`: fold region stops that share a city's point; ranged
  citations resolve to the arrival verse.
- Full map: the step-through replaces numbered badges; the confidence legend
  leaves the chapter-framed view. The parchment atlas task (cf9a983e) stays
  parked until the sea decision is made on the polygon fill, not the raster.
- Data: `scripts/data/journeys.yml` gap notes contain "--"; replace with
  plain punctuation when touched.
